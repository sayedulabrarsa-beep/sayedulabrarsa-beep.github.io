## 1. English: Expiry Date vs Manufacturing Date

**Existing URL:**  
`https://sayedulabrar.site/blog/expiry-date-vs-manufacturing-date-medicine/`

**Observed GSC:** 72 impressions · 0 clicks · 0% CTR · average position 8.97 (21–23 Nov 2026).

**Current page strengths:** direct answer; EXP/MFG/batch number; month/year decoder; date timeline; after-opening limits; storage rules; 4-step pack check; quiz; myths; FAQs; references. The page already contains Bangladesh DGDA guidance. Do not add a duplicate DGDA section.

### Proposed title
Medicine Expiry Date: What EXP and MFG Mean | Sayedul Abrar

### Proposed meta description
Learn what EXP, MFG and batch numbers mean on medicine packs. Understand month/year expiry dates, use-after-opening limits and safe storage.

### Content edits to consider
1. Bring the Bangladesh DGDA month/year rule and official source closer to the first answer, if not already prominent enough.
2. Make clear that the month/year interpretation applies to a printed expiry date that contains only month and year, and that product-specific label/leaflet instructions remain essential.
3. Distinguish the printed expiry date from any shorter use-after-opening, reconstitution or dilution period.
4. Do not suggest all opened eye drops, syrups or creams have the same in-use period.
5. Ensure all sample date formats are clearly labelled and avoid ambiguous numeric dates.

### Suggested concise direct answer
The manufacturing date (MFG) tells you when a medicine batch was made. The expiry date (EXP) tells you the last date through which the manufacturer expects the product to remain within its specifications when stored as directed. A month-and-year-only expiry date is interpreted under the applicable labelling guidance; in Bangladesh, the DGDA guideline says it means the last day of that month. Some products have a shorter use-after-opening or after-reconstitution period.
