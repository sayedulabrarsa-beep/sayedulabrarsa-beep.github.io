Finix 10 is the 10 mg delayed-release sprinkle capsule of rabeprazole sodium, a proton pump inhibitor, in the Finix range from Opsonin Pharma.

Indications
  • Healing of erosive or ulcerative gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD)
  • Maintenance of healing of erosive or ulcerative GERD
  • Treatment of symptomatic GERD

Also described in general rabeprazole references:

  • Short-term treatment of active duodenal ulcer
  • Zollinger–Ellison syndrome and other pathological hypersecretory conditions
  • H. pylori eradication only as triple therapy with two antibiotics, never as monotherapy
  • Active benign gastric ulcer and prevention of NSAID-associated gastric ulcers (guideline-supported; labelling varies by product)
Dosage & administration
  • Open the sprinkle capsule and scatter the contents on a small amount of soft food or liquid. Take it at once, within 15 minutes, without chewing the granules, about 30 minutes before a meal.
  • Check the pack insert for any form-specific instruction.

Doses are general rabeprazole reference values. Always follow the prescriber and the current insert.

How it works

Rabeprazole irreversibly inhibits the gastric H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (proton pump) on parietal cells, blocking the final step of acid secretion. Acid control lasts until new pumps are made, even though plasma half-life is only about 1–2 hours. It is absorbed in the small intestine (peak about 2–5 h, bioavailability about 52%) and metabolised in the liver, with less dependence on CYP2C19 than some other PPIs.

Interactions
  • Rilpivirine-containing HIV medicines: contraindicated, because absorption falls sharply.
  • Atazanavir and some other HIV protease inhibitors: not recommended together.
  • Warfarin: INR / PT rises have been reported, so monitor at start and stop.
  • High-dose methotrexate: levels may rise and persist, so a physician may pause the PPI.
  • Drugs that need stomach acid to be absorbed (ketoconazole, itraconazole, some iron salts, erlotinib, mycophenolate): absorption may fall.
  • Digoxin: absorption may increase, so watch for toxicity.
  • Clopidogrel: minimal effect on its active metabolite; no routine adjustment, clinical judgement applies.

Tell the doctor or pharmacist about all medicines, supplements and herbal products.

Contraindications
  • Known hypersensitivity to rabeprazole, other substituted benzimidazoles or any component.
  • Use with rilpivirine-containing antiretrovirals.
Side effects

Usually mild and short-lived: headache, diarrhoea (less often constipation), abdominal pain, nausea or vomiting, flatulence and dry mouth. In H. pylori combination therapy, diarrhoea and taste disturbance are reported more often. Seek care for severe or persistent symptoms, allergic reactions or unexplained diarrhoea.

Precautions & warnings
  • Symptom relief does not exclude gastric malignancy. Weight loss, recurrent vomiting, difficulty swallowing or GI bleeding need assessment.
  • Long-term, high-dose use is linked with fractures, low magnesium (over about a year) and reduced vitamin B12 absorption (over about 3 years).
  • May raise the risk of Clostridioides difficile diarrhoea. Persistent diarrhoea needs prompt review.
  • Acute interstitial nephritis and, rarely, lupus-type reactions have been reported with PPIs.
  • Stopping after prolonged use can cause rebound acid symptoms. Taper under physician guidance.
  • Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest appropriate time.
Pregnancy, lactation & special populations

Human pregnancy data are limited, so use only if clearly needed and the benefit outweighs the risk. It is not definitively known whether it passes into breast milk, so take advice before using it while breastfeeding.

No routine dose change for mild-to-moderate renal impairment, mild hepatic impairment or age alone. Take physician guidance in significant hepatic impairment. Paediatric GERD (physician-supervised only): ages 1–11, 5 mg daily under 15 kg or 10 mg daily from 15 kg, up to 12 weeks; 12 years and older, 20 mg daily for up to 8 weeks. Not established under 1 year.

Overdose & storage

Overdose. Experience is limited and there is no specific antidote. Treatment is supportive. Seek immediate medical help or poison control.

Storage. Store below 30 °C, away from light and moisture. Keep out of reach of children.

Is this a medicine for ordinary gas or bloating?
No. A PPI lowers stomach acid and is used for specific acid-related conditions. A clinician should assess persistent or severe symptoms.

What if a dose is missed?
Take it when remembered unless the next dose is close. Do not double up.

Finix HP is a combination pack of rabeprazole, amoxicillin and clarithromycin. The manufacturer lists it for Helicobacter pylori eradication in specified ulcer conditions. It is not for routine acidity, gas or bloating.

Composition
  • Rabeprazole Sodium BP 20 mg tablet
  • Amoxicillin BP 500 mg capsule
  • Clarithromycin USP 500 mg tablet

The manufacturer insert describes seven sachets per carton, with one combination blister in each sachet.

Manufacturer-listed use

Eradication of H. pylori in active chronic gastric and duodenal ulcer disease, as stated in the manufacturer's product information.

How the components work

Rabeprazole inhibits the gastric H⁺/K⁺-ATPase proton pump and reduces acid secretion. Amoxicillin interferes with bacterial cell-wall synthesis. Clarithromycin inhibits bacterial protein synthesis through the 50S ribosomal subunit.

Prescribing considerations

Suitability depends on confirmed diagnosis, allergy history, previous antibiotic exposure, interactions, organ function and local antimicrobial resistance. Clarithromycin-containing regimens may not suit every setting. A qualified prescriber must choose the regimen. Do not self-start, share or reuse this combination.

Quick answers

Can it be taken for ordinary gas or heartburn?
No. It contains two antibiotics and needs a diagnosis and a prescription.

Does everyone with H. pylori need the same treatment?
No. A clinician weighs resistance, prior antibiotics, allergies, interactions and patient factors.

Malifa 30 is a ferric maltol 30 mg capsule, an oral iron preparation listed by Navana Pharmaceuticals for the treatment of iron deficiency in adults. It is part of my earlier work as a Brand Executive.

Indication

Treatment of iron deficiency in adults (Bangladesh listing).

Some other regional labels describe iron-deficiency anaemia and include patients from 10 years of age. Follow the local approved insert.

Dosage & administration
  • 30 mg twice daily, preferably on an empty stomach, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals.
  • Do not open, break or chew the capsule.
  • Treatment is generally at least 12 weeks and continues until ferritin is in the normal range.
  • Paediatric: safety and effectiveness are not established in the Bangladesh listing. Some other labels differ, so follow the local insert.
How it works

Ferric maltol delivers iron for uptake across the intestinal wall and transfer to transferrin and ferritin. It has been shown to raise serum iron parameters, including ferritin and transferrin saturation (TSAT). It is described as a ferric–maltol complex that limits free iron in the gut.

Interactions
  • Dimercaprol: may raise the risk of nephrotoxicity, so avoid the combination.
  • Some oral drugs (mycophenolate, ethinyl estradiol, ciprofloxacin, doxycycline): bioavailability may fall. Separate doses by at least 4 hours where it matters.
  • Levothyroxine, bisphosphonates, penicillamine: iron may reduce absorption, so separate the doses.
  • Antacids and PPIs: may reduce absorption of iron by altering gastric pH. Avoid together where possible.
  • Food: reduces iron bioavailability. Dairy, tea and coffee do so too.
Contraindications
  • Known hypersensitivity to ferric maltol or any component.
  • History of haemochromatosis or other iron-overload syndromes.
  • Patients receiving repeated blood transfusions.
  • Anaemia not caused by iron deficiency (per general reference).
Side effects

Most common: flatulence, diarrhoea, constipation, discoloured (dark) faeces, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting. Dark stools can interfere with occult-blood stool tests.

Precautions & warnings
  • Avoid during an active inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) flare.
  • Do not give with evidence of iron overload or with intravenous iron.
  • Assess iron parameters before starting and monitor during therapy.
  • Accidental iron overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6.
Pregnancy, lactation & special populations

After oral use ferric maltol is not absorbed systemically as an intact complex, so fetal exposure and infant exposure through milk are not expected. There are no data on human milk presence, effects on the breastfed child or on milk production. No well-established dose change is defined for renal or hepatic impairment, so use physician oversight.

Overdose & storage

Overdose. No overdose data are available for Malifa. Early signs of iron overload include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and diarrhoea, and serious cases can progress to systemic toxicity. Seek immediate help.

Storage. Cool place below 25 °C (listing), dry and protected from light. Some references quote below 30 °C, so follow the pack. Keep out of reach of children.
