Onset, Peak and Duration of Drug Action Explained

Understand when a medicine starts working, when its effect is strongest and how long it lasts.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 15 November 2026

In simple terms: Understand when a medicine starts working, when its effect is strongest and how long it lasts.

1. Onset of action

Onset is the time from administration until a medicine begins to produce a noticeable or measurable effect. It depends on the route, formulation, absorption, distribution and how much drug is needed at the target site.

2. Peak effect

Peak effect is the time or point when the medicine's effect is greatest after a dose. Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) is a related but different measure: the highest measured concentration in plasma does not always occur at exactly the same time as the strongest clinical effect.

3. Duration of action

Duration is how long a medicine produces its intended effect. It may depend on how long the drug stays above an effective concentration, receptor binding, active metabolites, irreversible effects and the body's response. Duration is not always the same as plasma half-life.

4. Why do two formulations differ?

A liquid, immediate-release tablet, delayed-release product and extended-release tablet can have different absorption profiles. A faster onset is not automatically better; the right formulation depends on the medicine and treatment goal.

5. Practical safety

Do not take an extra dose because you do not feel an effect immediately. Some medicines take time to work, and taking more can cause harm. Follow the label and prescriber's instructions.

6. Key takeaways

Onset describes when an effect begins; peak describes its greatest effect; duration describes how long it lasts. These are related concepts but are not interchangeable with plasma concentration or half-life.

Remember

Frequently asked questions

Do all medicines behave in the same way?

No. Effects depend on the ingredient, formulation, route and the patient's circumstances.

Can I change a medicine dose myself?

No. Follow the prescription or label and consult a doctor or pharmacist before making changes.
