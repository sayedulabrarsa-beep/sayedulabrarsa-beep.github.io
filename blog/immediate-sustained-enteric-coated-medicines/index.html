Immediate-Release vs Sustained-Release vs Enteric-Coated Medicines

Learn how medicine release designs differ and why modified-release tablets should not be crushed without advice.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 2 December 2026

In simple terms: Learn how medicine release designs differ and why modified-release tablets should not be crushed without advice.

1. Immediate-release

Immediate-release (IR) medicines release their active ingredient without a special mechanism intended to delay or extend release. The medicine still needs to dissolve and be absorbed, so immediate-release does not mean instant action.

2. Sustained or extended-release

Sustained-release (SR), extended-release (ER/XR) and controlled-release products are designed to release medicine over a longer period. This may reduce how often a dose is needed, but the exact design and naming vary between products.

3. Delayed-release and enteric-coated

Delayed-release products release the active ingredient later. Enteric coating is one method: it resists the acidic stomach environment and is designed to dissolve at a higher pH in the intestine. It may protect the drug from stomach acid or protect the stomach from local irritation.

4. How to recognise them

Labels may use abbreviations such as SR, CR, ER, XR, XL, MR, DR or EC, but naming is not perfectly standardised. Always check the specific product information rather than guessing from an abbreviation.

5. Can you crush or split them?

Some modified-release tablets must not be crushed or chewed because this can release too much medicine at once. Enteric-coated products may lose their protective function if crushed. Some capsules can be opened and their pellets sprinkled, but only if the product instructions specifically allow it.

6. Key takeaways

IR, sustained-release and enteric-coated describe different release behaviours. They are not interchangeable by appearance alone. Check the leaflet or ask a pharmacist before splitting, crushing or opening any medicine.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between immediate-release and sustained-release medicine?

Immediate-release products release the active ingredient without a design intended to prolong release. Sustained- or extended-release products are designed to release it over a longer period. The exact design varies by product.

Is enteric-coated the same as sustained-release?

No. Enteric coating delays release until the product reaches a suitable intestinal environment. Sustained-release design extends the release of the active ingredient over time. They serve different purposes.

Can I crush an enteric-coated or modified-release tablet?

Do not crush, chew or split these products unless the specific product information says it is safe. Altering the dosage form can change release or damage the coating. Ask a pharmacist if unsure.
