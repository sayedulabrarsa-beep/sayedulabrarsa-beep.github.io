Expiry Date vs Manufacturing Date: How to Read Any Medicine Pack in 10 Seconds

Expiry date vs manufacturing date on medicine explained: read EXP, MFG and batch number, the month-year rule, storage tips, myths and FAQs.

What is the difference between expiry date and manufacturing date?

Quick answer: The manufacturing date (MFG) is when the medicine was made. The expiry date (EXP) is the last date the manufacturer guarantees the medicine's full strength and safety, if stored as directed. If the pack shows only a month and year, such as 07/2027, it is good through the last day of that month. Do not use a medicine after its expiry date.

Your medicine pack is hiding a tiny clock

Turn over any medicine pack and you will find a tiny stamped clock near an edge or on the bottom. Three short codes tell the story: MFG (made), EXP (expires) and B.No. (which batch).

Look at the edge of a strip, the end flap of a box, or the bottom or label of a bottle. Use good light; if you cannot read the date, ask your pharmacist instead of guessing.

What is a manufacturing date (MFG)?

The manufacturing date is printed as MFG, Mfg. Date or Manu. Date. It tells you when that batch was made. It is not a deadline. Use it to work out how old a pack is, but decide whether to use the medicine by its expiry date and storage—not by how new it looks.

What is an expiry date (EXP) and what does 07/2027 mean?

The expiry date is printed as EXP, Exp. Date, Use before or Use by. It is the last date the manufacturer guarantees the medicine's full strength and safety when stored as directed. Manufacturers set it using stability testing.

The month-year rule: If only a month and year are printed, the medicine is good through the last day of that month. A pack marked 07/2027 is good through 31 July 2027.

What is a batch number?

The batch number appears as B.No., Batch No. or Lot. It identifies the production batch. If there is a quality problem or recall, it helps trace affected packs. If you report a medicine problem, keep the pack or note the batch number, MFG date and EXP date.

How the dates fit together: the two-clock timeline

The time between MFG and EXP is the medicine's shelf life. It varies by product. Eye drops, syrups, powders mixed with water and creams in jars can have a shorter use-after-opening limit. If that limit comes sooner than the printed EXP, the sooner date wins.

Can I use medicine after the expiry date?

Short answer: no. After the expiry date the manufacturer can no longer guarantee strength or safety. A medicine may lose strength, and you cannot tell by looking or smelling. Old liquids, eye drops, injections and medicines for serious conditions can carry extra risk.

Ask your pharmacy how to dispose of expired medicine safely, and keep it out of children's reach until it is gone.

How to store medicine at home: 5 rules

The expiry date only holds if medicine is stored as directed. Heat, humidity and light can spoil it earlier.

  1. Keep it in the original pack with the leaflet, so dates and instructions stay with it.
  2. Choose a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight.
  3. Avoid bathrooms, stoves, sunny windowsills and parked cars.
  4. Follow the label. If it says below 30°C, protect from light or refrigerate, follow it.
  5. Keep it out of children's reach with lids closed, ideally in a locked box.

The 4-step medicine pack check

Before taking medicine, spend ten seconds on these four steps:

  1. Find EXP. Apply the month-year rule.
  2. Check the after-opening limit. Look for a use-within line on drops, syrups, mixed liquids and jar creams.
  3. Look at the pack and medicine. A broken seal, damage, melting, colour change or strange smell is a reason to stop.
  4. Ask when unsure. Your pharmacist can check a date you cannot read.

While you have the pack in hand, also read the generic name and brand name on the label.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between expiry date and manufacturing date?

MFG is when the medicine was made. EXP is the last date the manufacturer guarantees full strength and safety if stored as directed.

What does EXP 07/2027 mean on a medicine?

It is good through 31 July 2027.

Can I use medicine after the expiry date?

No. Replace it and ask your pharmacist if unsure.

What does the batch number on medicine mean?

It identifies the production batch so packs can be traced during a quality problem or recall.

How long is a medicine good after opening?

It depends on the product. Follow the label's shorter use-after-opening limit; ask a pharmacist if unclear.

Where is the expiry date on a medicine pack?

Usually on a strip edge, box flap, or bottle bottom/label. Check in good light.

Why do medicines expire?

The active ingredient can break down over time, especially with heat, humidity and light.

How should I dispose of expired medicine?

Ask your pharmacy about safe disposal and keep it away from children until then.

Safety check

Do not use a medicine after expiry, if the pack is damaged, or if it looks, smells or feels wrong. A medicine that looks fine may still have lost strength. Ask a pharmacist or doctor if unsure about a date or storage rule.

Key takeaways

  • MFG = when it was made. EXP = the last trusted date.
  • Month and year only? Good through the last day of that month.
  • Opened drops, syrups and jar creams may have a shorter limit.
  • Store in the original pack, cool and dry, away from sun and children.
  • Never guess an unreadable date. Ask a pharmacist.
