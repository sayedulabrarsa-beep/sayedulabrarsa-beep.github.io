How Medicines Work in the Body: The Complete Series

Follow one medicine from the moment you swallow it to the moment it leaves your body, in seven short articles, in English and Bangla.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 28 November 2026

In simple terms: Pharmacists describe the journey of a medicine with four steps called ADME: Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism and Excretion. Three more ideas explain timing and amount: half-life, bioavailability, and onset, peak and duration. Read them in this order.
In this article
  1. Read the series in order
  2. Why this order?
  3. Frequently asked questions

Read the series in order

  1. Drug Absorption: How Does Medicine Enter the Bloodstream?How a medicine gets from the gut into the blood, and why routes differ.
  2. Drug Distribution: How Medicines Reach Their TargetBlood as the highway, protein binding and the blood–brain barrier.
  3. Drug Metabolism: How Does the Body Process Medicines?The liver, CYP enzymes, first-pass metabolism and prodrugs.
  4. Drug Excretion: How Do Medicines Leave the Body?Kidneys, bile and other exits, and why kidney health matters.
  5. Drug Half-Life: How Long Does a Medicine Stay in the Body?The 4–5 half-lives rule, dosing times and steady state.
  6. Bioavailability: How Much Medicine Reaches the Bloodstream?Why IV is 100%, food effects and bioequivalence of generics.
  7. Onset, Peak and Duration of Drug Action ExplainedThe time curve, the therapeutic window and why not to double a dose.

Why this order?

The first four articles follow the medicine itself: it enters the blood (absorption), spreads through the body (distribution), is changed, mostly in the liver (metabolism), and leaves, mostly through the kidneys (excretion). Together these are called ADME.

The last three use that journey to answer everyday questions: how long a medicine stays (half-life), how much of a dose actually arrives (bioavailability), and when it starts and stops working (onset, peak and duration).

If you are new to the topic, start with How Do Medicines Work Inside the Human Body? for the big picture.

Frequently asked questions

What does ADME stand for?

ADME stands for Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism and Excretion. These four steps describe how a medicine enters the body, spreads, is changed and leaves.

Do I need to read the articles in order?

No, each article stands on its own. Reading in order makes the later ideas, like half-life and bioavailability, easier to follow.

Is the series available in Bangla?

Yes. Every article in the series has a full Bangla version; use the language switch at the top of each page.
