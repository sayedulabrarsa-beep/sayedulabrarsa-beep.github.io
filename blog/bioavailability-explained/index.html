Bioavailability: How Much Medicine Reaches the Bloodstream?

Learn absolute and relative bioavailability, first-pass metabolism and why route and formulation matter.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 15 November 2026

In simple terms: Learn absolute and relative bioavailability, first-pass metabolism and why route and formulation matter.

1. What is bioavailability?

Bioavailability is the rate and extent to which an active drug reaches the systemic circulation. It helps describe how much of a dose becomes available in the blood and how quickly it gets there.

2. Why is intravenous bioavailability 100%?

An intravenous dose is placed directly into the systemic circulation, so its bioavailability is considered 100%. An oral dose must first be released from its dosage form, dissolve, cross the gut wall and may undergo metabolism in the gut or liver before reaching systemic blood.

3. Absolute and relative bioavailability

Absolute bioavailability compares a non-intravenous route with an intravenous reference dose. Relative bioavailability compares two formulations or products, usually by measuring exposure under suitable study conditions. These comparisons are used in pharmacokinetic and bioequivalence assessments.

4. What affects bioavailability?

Absorption, formulation, food, gastrointestinal conditions, transporters and first-pass metabolism can all affect exposure. A change in dosage form or route may change how much and how quickly the active ingredient reaches circulation.

5. Bioavailability is not the same as effectiveness

A higher measured exposure does not automatically mean a medicine is better for every patient. Clinical effect also depends on dose, target, concentration over time, safety and the condition being treated. Use medicines as prescribed and do not substitute formulations without professional advice.

6. Key takeaways

Bioavailability describes the rate and extent of systemic availability. IV administration is the reference at 100%; oral bioavailability may be lower because of incomplete absorption and first-pass metabolism.

Frequently asked questions

Do all medicines behave in the same way?

No. Effects depend on the ingredient, formulation, route and the patient's circumstances.

Can I change a medicine dose myself?

No. Follow the prescription or label and consult a doctor or pharmacist before making changes.
