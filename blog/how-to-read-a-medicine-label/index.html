How to Read a Medicine Label: Strength, Dose, Expiry and Storage

A practical guide to reading a medicine pack, including generic name, strength, directions, warnings and expiry.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 15 November 2026

In simple terms: A practical guide to reading a medicine pack, including generic name, strength, directions, warnings and expiry.

1. Start with the medicine name

Look for the generic or active ingredient name and the brand name. The active ingredient tells you what medicine is in the product. Similar brand names can contain different ingredients, so never identify a medicine by colour or packaging alone.

2. Understand strength and dosage form

Strength tells you how much active ingredient is present in a unit or measured amount—for example, milligrams per tablet or milligrams per 5 mL. Check whether the product is a tablet, capsule, liquid, cream or another form. Strength is not the same as the dose you should take.

3. Read directions carefully

The label or prescription should explain how much to take, how often, by which route and for how long. For liquids, use the supplied measuring device. Do not use another person's prescription or change the dose yourself.

4. Check dates and storage

Find the manufacturing and expiry dates and read storage instructions. Keep medicines in their original packaging when possible, away from excessive heat, moisture and direct sunlight unless the label says otherwise. Keep them out of children's reach.

5. Warnings and special instructions

Look for food instructions, allergy warnings, age restrictions, pregnancy or breastfeeding advice, driving cautions and interactions. Modified-release or enteric-coated medicines may have special handling instructions. Ask a pharmacist if any wording is unclear.

6. A quick label checklist

Confirm the medicine name, strength, dosage form, route, dose directions, expiry date, storage and warnings before use. If the pack is damaged, the label is unreadable or the medicine looks different from what you expect, ask a pharmacist before taking it.

7. Key takeaways

A medicine label contains essential information for safe use. Check the active ingredient, strength, directions, expiry, storage and warnings every time you receive a new product.

Frequently asked questions

What information should I check on a medicine label?

Check the generic and brand names, strength, dosage form, directions, warnings, expiry date, storage instructions, batch number and manufacturer details.

What do MFG and EXP mean?

MFG is the manufacturing date; EXP is the expiry date. Do not use a medicine beyond its expiry date, and follow the pack instructions where only a month and year are shown.

What should I do if the label or dose instruction is unclear?

Do not guess or copy another person's dose. Ask the dispensing pharmacist or prescriber to explain the specific product and instructions before using it.
