Prescription (Rx) vs OTC Medicines in Bangladesh: What Is the Difference?

What prescription-only and over-the-counter mean, what the law in Bangladesh says, why antibiotics carry a red label, and how to use your pharmacist well.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 26 November 2026

In Bangladesh, the law allows sale without a registered physician's prescription only for OTC medicines; antibiotics always need a prescription.

1. What do Rx and OTC mean?

The U.S. FDA gives a simple description. Prescription medicines are prescribed by a doctor, bought at a pharmacy, and meant for one person. Over-the-counter (OTC) medicines do not need a doctor's prescription.

OTC does not mean "harmless". It means the medicine is considered suitable for self-care when the label is followed. Taking too much, taking it for too long, or mixing it with other medicines can still cause harm.

2. What the law says in Bangladesh

The Drugs and Cosmetics Act 2023 (ঔষধ ও কসমেটিকস আইন, ২০২৩), section 40, says that, apart from OTC medicines, no antibiotic or other medicine may be sold, stocked or displayed for sale without a registered physician's prescription. Section 45 says retail sale of allopathic medicine must be under the personal supervision of a pharmacist, diploma pharmacist or pharmacy technician. WHO reports that breaking the prescription rule can bring a fine of up to Tk 20,000.

Which medicines count as OTC? The National Drug Policy 2016 says the OTC medicines have been published in its appendices. A 2020 report in The Business Standard put the number at 39 medicines. The list itself is short: most medicines on a pharmacy shelf are prescription medicines.

Reality check: studies show the law is not always followed. A 2021 survey of 128 pharmacies in Bangladesh found that about half (50.9%) of antibiotic courses were sold without a registered physician's prescription (Islam et al., 2022). A pharmacy selling a medicine without a prescription does not make it an OTC medicine.

3. Why antibiotics need a prescription

WHO says the misuse and overuse of antimicrobials drive drug-resistant infections, and bacterial resistance was associated with more than 4.7 million deaths in 2021. A doctor checks whether you need an antibiotic at all (most colds and flu are viral), and which one, at what dose and for how long. Read more in Why You Must Complete an Antibiotic Course.

4. The red antibiotic label

To help everyone spot antibiotics, Bangladesh's Directorate General of Drug Administration (DGDA) requires antibiotic packs to carry a red marking, the word "Antibiotic" and a warning not to use without the prescription of a registered physician. WHO Bangladesh reports that the rule was endorsed in March 2022, launched in November 2022 and enforced from December 2022, for both human and animal medicines. If you see the red marking, the medicine needs a prescription.

5. Using OTC medicines safely

  • Read the label every time. Products with similar names can contain different active ingredients (FDA).
  • Check the active ingredient. Some prescription medicines contain the same ingredients as OTC medicines, so you could take more than you should (MedlinePlus). See Generic Name vs Brand Name.
  • Do not take more, or for longer, than the label says. If symptoms continue, see a doctor.
  • Do not guess a child's dose. Ask a doctor or pharmacist.
  • Check the dates and storage. See Expiry Date vs Manufacturing Date.

6. How to ask your pharmacist

The pharmacist is the medicine expert you can reach without an appointment. Useful questions:

  1. Is this a prescription medicine or an OTC medicine?
  2. What is the active ingredient, and is it already in anything else I take?
  3. How much, how often, and before or after food?
  4. What side effects should make me stop and see a doctor?
  5. Is it safe for me if I am pregnant, breastfeeding, or have kidney, liver or heart problems?

Tell the pharmacist about every medicine, herbal product and supplement you take. DGDA's model pharmacy scheme, started in 2016, places graduate (A-grade) pharmacists in model pharmacies for this reason.

7. Key takeaways

The OTC list is short; most medicines need a prescription. Antibiotics always do. Read the label, check the active ingredient, and ask the pharmacist when in doubt.

  • A prescription medicine is chosen for one person by a registered doctor.
  • In Bangladesh, only medicines on the official OTC list may be sold without a prescription.
  • Antibiotics always need a registered physician's prescription; look for the red label.
  • Read the label and check the active ingredient to avoid double dosing.
  • Ask the pharmacist, especially for children, pregnancy or when you take other medicines.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between prescription and OTC medicines?

A prescription medicine is chosen by a registered doctor for one person and dispensed against that prescription. An OTC medicine can be bought without a prescription for minor, short-term problems when the label is followed.

Is there an official OTC medicine list in Bangladesh?

Yes. The National Drug Policy 2016 says the OTC medicines are published in its appendices. A 2020 news report counted 39 medicines on the list. All other medicines need a registered physician's prescription.

Can a pharmacy sell antibiotics without a prescription in Bangladesh?

No. The Drugs and Cosmetics Act 2023 does not allow antibiotics or other non-OTC medicines to be sold without a registered physician's prescription.

What does the red label on antibiotic packs mean?

Since 2022, DGDA has required antibiotic packs to show a red marking, the word Antibiotic and a warning not to use without the prescription of a registered physician.

Are OTC medicines completely safe?

No medicine is completely safe. OTC medicines are suitable for self-care when used as the label says, but taking too much, for too long or with other medicines can cause harm. Ask a pharmacist if you are unsure.
