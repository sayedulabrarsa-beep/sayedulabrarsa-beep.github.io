Side Effects vs Adverse Drug Reactions: What Is the Difference?

Learn the difference between side effects, adverse drug reactions, serious reactions and what to do.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 15 November 2026

In simple terms: Learn the difference between side effects, adverse drug reactions, serious reactions and what to do.

1. What is a side effect?

A side effect is an unintended effect that occurs in addition to the intended therapeutic effect when a medicine is used. It may be mild, bothersome or clinically important. Whether it occurs can depend on the medicine, dose and individual.

2. What is an adverse drug reaction?

An adverse drug reaction (ADR) is a harmful or unintended response to a medicine used at normal doses for prevention, diagnosis or treatment. In everyday language, people sometimes use side effect and ADR interchangeably, but pharmacovigilance uses more specific definitions.

3. Are all reactions allergies?

No. An allergic reaction is an immune-mediated response and is only one type of adverse reaction. Many medicine-related problems are not allergies—for example, stomach irritation, dizziness or dose-related effects. Do not label a medicine allergy without discussing the reaction with a healthcare professional.

4. When is it urgent?

Seek emergency help for difficulty breathing, swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, fainting, or a rapidly spreading severe rash after taking a medicine. For other new or concerning symptoms, contact a doctor or pharmacist promptly. Do not take another dose if you suspect a serious reaction until you have received urgent professional advice.

5. Report and document reactions

Record the medicine name, dose, time taken, symptoms, onset and any action taken. Keep the packaging if possible. A healthcare professional can assess whether the medicine caused the reaction and whether it should be documented or reported through the appropriate pharmacovigilance system.

6. Key takeaways

Side effects are unintended effects; ADRs are harmful or unintended responses at normal use. Not every reaction is an allergy. Recognise emergency symptoms and seek professional assessment.

Remember

Side effects are unintended effects; ADRs are harmful or unintended responses at normal use. Not every reaction is an allergy. Recognise emergency symptoms and seek professional assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Do all medicines behave in the same way?

No. Effects depend on the ingredient, formulation, route and the patient's circumstances.

Can I change a medicine dose myself?

No. Follow the prescription or label and consult a doctor or pharmacist before making changes.
