Drug Interactions: When Medicines, Food and Supplements Affect Each Other

Understand medicine–medicine, medicine–food and medicine–condition interactions and how to reduce risk.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 15 November 2026

In simple terms: Understand medicine–medicine, medicine–food and medicine–condition interactions and how to reduce risk.

1. What is a drug interaction?

A drug interaction happens when another medicine, food, drink, supplement or health condition changes how a medicine works or changes the risk of side effects. The result may be stronger, weaker or different from the expected effect.

2. Medicine–medicine interactions

Two medicines may affect each other's absorption, metabolism, distribution or elimination. They may also have similar effects that add together, such as two medicines that both cause drowsiness or bleeding risk. Some combinations are intentional; others need monitoring or should be avoided.

3. Food and drink interactions

Food can change the absorption of certain medicines. Some medicines have specific instructions about meals. Grapefruit can affect enzymes or transporters for selected medicines, while alcohol can increase drowsiness with some drugs. These effects are medicine-specific, not universal.

4. Supplements and health conditions

Herbal products and supplements can interact with prescribed medicines. Kidney or liver disease, dehydration and other conditions may also change how a medicine is handled. Tell your healthcare professional about all prescription medicines, OTC products and supplements you use.

5. How can you reduce risk?

Keep an up-to-date medicine list, read the label and patient leaflet, and ask a pharmacist before adding a new medicine or supplement. Do not stop a prescribed medicine because of an interaction warning without professional advice.

6. Key takeaways

Interactions can change a medicine's effect or safety. They involve medicines, foods, supplements and health conditions. A complete medicine list and pharmacist review can help identify avoidable risks.

Frequently asked questions

Do all medicines behave in the same way?

No. Effects depend on the ingredient, formulation, route and the patient's circumstances.

Can I change a medicine dose myself?

No. Follow the prescription or label and consult a doctor or pharmacist before making changes.
