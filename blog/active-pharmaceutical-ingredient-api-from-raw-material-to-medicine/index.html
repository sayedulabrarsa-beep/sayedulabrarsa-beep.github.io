Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API): From Raw Material to Medicine

Understand the active substance behind a medicine—and the quality steps between raw materials and a finished product.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

The simple answer: An active pharmaceutical ingredient (API), also called a drug substance, is the substance in a medicine that provides the intended pharmacological activity or other direct effect. A tablet or capsule usually contains an API together with other ingredients that help form and deliver the medicine.

When you look at a tablet, you see the finished dosage form. But before that tablet is manufactured, its active ingredient must be produced or sourced, purified, tested and released under controlled quality systems. That active ingredient is the API.

1. What exactly is an API?

The International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) and the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) describe an API as a substance or mixture of substances intended for use in manufacturing a drug product that becomes the active ingredient of that product. It is intended to provide pharmacological activity or another direct effect in diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or function of the body.

For example, in a conventional paracetamol tablet, paracetamol is the API. The tablet also contains excipients—ingredients that support manufacturing, stability, disintegration, appearance or other product characteristics. Excipients are not interchangeable with the API: they serve different functions.

2. API vs finished pharmaceutical product

An API is the active drug substance. A finished pharmaceutical product (FPP) is the medicine in its final dosage form, such as a tablet, capsule, oral liquid or injection, ready for the relevant packaging and supply steps.

  • API: the active substance that provides the intended pharmacological effect.
  • Excipients: other formulation ingredients used for functions such as binding, filling, coating, preservation or helping the dosage form perform as designed.
  • FPP: the formulated medicine containing the API and excipients in a defined dosage form and strength.

One company may manufacture the API and another may formulate it into tablets. Some organisations perform both activities. The business arrangement does not change the need to control the quality of the materials and processes.

3. How is an API manufactured?

There is no single manufacturing route for every API. ICH Q7 recognises several broad approaches, including chemical synthesis, extraction, fermentation or cell culture, and recovery from natural sources. The appropriate process depends on the substance and its chemistry or biology.

A simplified manufacturing journey

Step 1: Starting materials

Manufacturing begins with defined starting materials, reagents, solvents or biological inputs. Their identity, quality and suitability must be assessed. Supplier qualification, specifications, sampling and traceability help prevent unsuitable materials from entering production.

ICH Q7 expects the manufacturer to define and document the point at which API manufacturing begins. The API starting material is normally identified and justified in the regulatory filing. Q7 does not apply identically to every step before that defined point, although appropriate controls are still expected for producing the starting material. As the process moves toward the final API, purification, physical processing and packaging, GMP controls become progressively more stringent.

Step 2: Production and process control

In chemical synthesis, controlled reactions convert starting materials through one or more intermediates into the desired substance. Other APIs may be produced through extraction, fermentation or cell-based processes. Manufacturers monitor relevant parameters such as temperature, time, pH, mixing or other process-specific variables. The exact controls depend on the process.

Step 3: Isolation and purification

The desired substance is separated from process mixtures and unwanted materials. Depending on the process, operations may include filtration, crystallisation, washing, drying or other purification techniques. The aim is to obtain material that meets its defined quality requirements.

Step 4: Physical processing and packaging

Some APIs undergo milling, micronisation, blending or other physical processing to achieve specified properties. The API is then packed in suitable containers with appropriate identification and storage controls to protect it during handling and transport.

4. How do manufacturers know an API is suitable?

Testing is performed against approved or otherwise established specifications. The precise tests depend on the API and its intended use, but may include:

  • Identity: confirms that the material is the intended substance.
  • Assay or content: measures the amount or strength of the API.
  • Related substances and impurities: evaluates specified and other relevant impurities.
  • Residual solvents: checks solvents that may remain from manufacturing.
  • Water content and physical properties: assessed when relevant to the substance and its specifications.
  • Microbiological quality: tested where required by the API type and intended use.

Passing one test does not automatically establish overall quality. Results must be evaluated against the complete specification, relevant procedures and batch documentation. A designated quality unit reviews the appropriate records and makes the release or rejection decision. The quality unit is responsible for API release and oversight of test results, but ICH Q7 does not require the quality unit itself to perform every laboratory test. Testing may be carried out by suitably trained personnel under appropriate laboratory controls. FDA's 2018 Q7 Questions and Answers clarifies this distinction; it is a US guidance example, not a substitute for Bangladesh requirements.

5. Why does GMP matter in API manufacturing?

Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) is a system of controls designed to help ensure that materials and products are consistently produced and controlled according to appropriate quality standards. ICH Q7 provides GMP guidance specifically for APIs.

In practical terms, GMP includes trained personnel, suitable premises and equipment, written procedures, controlled production records, cleaning and maintenance, investigations of deviations, change control, laboratory controls, supplier oversight and quality-unit responsibilities.

GMP does not mean that errors are impossible. It means there should be systems to prevent problems where possible, detect them, investigate them, document decisions and protect product quality.

WHO's API GMP guidance defines manufacturing broadly to include receipt of materials, production, packaging and labelling, quality control, release, storage and distribution. GMP is therefore not just a final laboratory check; it is a connected system across the API supply chain.

6. Why does API quality matter to patients?

The quality of the API contributes to the quality of the finished medicine. If the identity, strength, impurity profile or other critical attributes are not controlled, the finished product may not meet its intended specifications. That is why API quality cannot be judged only by the appearance of the powder or by the reputation of a supplier.

Finished-product manufacturers also need to control formulation, manufacturing, packaging and stability. API quality is essential, but it is one part of a wider pharmaceutical quality system.

7. What about API manufacturing in Bangladesh?

Bangladesh has an established finished-medicine manufacturing sector and has also pursued greater local capacity for API production. The origin and supply route of an API can differ by substance and manufacturer; it should not be assumed that every API used locally is made domestically.

The Directorate General of Drug Administration (DGDA) is Bangladesh's national medicines regulatory authority. Its published responsibilities include licensing premises, regulatory inspections, registration and marketing authorisation, laboratory testing and post-market surveillance. The applicable requirements for a particular API or finished medicine depend on the activity, product and current Bangladesh law.

BIDA's current Pharmaceuticals & API sector page provides investment and industry context. Its market and import figures are time-specific; this article does not reuse them as timeless 2026 statistics. BIDA is an investment-information source, not the medicines regulator.

Key takeaways

  • API means active pharmaceutical ingredient; it is the active substance in a medicine.
  • The API is different from the finished tablet, capsule or liquid.
  • Manufacturing routes vary: chemical synthesis, extraction, fermentation and other processes may be used.
  • Purification, testing, documentation and GMP controls support API quality.
  • The quality of the API contributes to—but does not alone determine—the quality of the finished medicine.

Frequently asked questions

Is API the same as a drug?

In pharmaceutical manufacturing, API or drug substance refers to the active substance intended to provide the pharmacological effect. In everyday language, “drug” can have broader meanings.

Is an API the same as a tablet?

No. The API is the active substance. A tablet is a finished dosage form that usually contains the API plus excipients.

Can one company manufacture both API and tablets?

Yes. Some companies perform both API production and finished-product manufacturing, while others specialise in one activity or outsource part of the process.

Are all APIs made by chemical synthesis?

No. Depending on the substance, APIs may be produced through chemical synthesis, extraction, fermentation, cell culture or other suitable routes.

Why are API impurities tested?

Impurities can arise from starting materials, reactions, processing or storage. Testing and process controls help ensure that the API meets its defined quality requirements.

Does GMP guarantee that a medicine will never have a quality problem?

No system can guarantee that problems will never occur. GMP establishes controls for consistent production, detection, investigation and prevention of quality issues.
