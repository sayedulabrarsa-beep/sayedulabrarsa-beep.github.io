Routes of Drug Administration: Oral, Injection, Inhaled and More

Explore common routes of medicine administration and how each route affects delivery and absorption.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 15 November 2026

In simple terms: Explore common routes of medicine administration and how each route affects delivery and absorption.

1. What is a route of administration?

A route of administration is the path by which a medicine enters or is applied to the body. The route is selected according to the medicine, the condition, how quickly an effect is needed and the patient's circumstances.

2. Oral and sublingual

Oral medicines are swallowed and pass through the gastrointestinal tract. They usually need to dissolve and be absorbed; some undergo first-pass metabolism. Sublingual medicines are placed under the tongue and absorbed through the mouth lining, which can reduce first-pass metabolism for suitable drugs.

3. Intravenous, intramuscular and subcutaneous

Intravenous (IV) medicines are delivered directly into a vein. Intramuscular (IM) injections are placed into muscle, while subcutaneous injections are placed into the tissue beneath the skin. IM and subcutaneous medicines must move from the injection site into the circulation.

4. Inhaled, topical and transdermal

Inhaled medicines are delivered through the airways, often for local respiratory effects. Topical medicines are applied to a body surface, commonly for local action. Transdermal patches are designed to deliver medicine through the skin into systemic circulation.

5. Why route matters

Routes differ in onset, bioavailability, convenience, risks and technique. A medicine intended for one route must not be used by another route unless the product information explicitly permits it. Never inject an oral product or swallow an inhalation product.

6. Key takeaways

The route affects how a medicine reaches its site of action. Follow the exact route and technique on the prescription and label; ask a healthcare professional if the instructions are unclear.

Frequently asked questions

Do all medicines behave in the same way?

No. Effects depend on the ingredient, formulation, route and the patient's circumstances.

Can I change a medicine dose myself?

No. Follow the prescription or label and consult a doctor or pharmacist before making changes.
