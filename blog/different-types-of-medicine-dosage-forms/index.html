Different Types of Medicine Dosage Forms: Tablets, Capsules, Liquids and More

A practical guide to common dosage forms and why medicines are made in different forms.

Englishবাংলা

By Sayedul Abrar, pharmacist · 15 November 2026

In simple terms: A practical guide to common dosage forms and why medicines are made in different forms.

1. What is a dosage form?

A dosage form is the physical form in which a medicine is supplied, such as a tablet, capsule, liquid, cream, inhaler or injection. It combines the active ingredient with other ingredients to make the medicine usable, stable and suitable for its intended route.

2. Solid oral forms

Tablets are compressed units that may be immediate-release, delayed-release or modified-release. Capsules contain powder, granules, pellets or liquid inside a shell. Some tablets dissolve in the mouth, while chewable tablets are designed to be chewed. These forms are not automatically interchangeable.

3. Liquid forms

Solutions contain drug dissolved in a liquid; suspensions contain particles dispersed through a liquid and may need shaking as directed. Syrups and oral drops can help when swallowing solid forms is difficult, but accurate measuring is essential. Use the supplied measuring device rather than a household spoon.

4. Topical and inhaled forms

Creams, ointments, gels and lotions are applied to the skin. Some are intended for local action, while transdermal patches deliver medicine through the skin into the circulation. Inhalers and nebuliser solutions deliver medicine to the respiratory tract; correct technique affects delivery.

5. Why does the form matter?

The dosage form can influence release, absorption, convenience, stability and adherence. Modified-release or enteric-coated products may be damaged by crushing or chewing. Do not split, crush, open or mix a medicine unless its product instructions or a pharmacist confirms it is suitable.

6. Key takeaways

Dosage forms are designed for specific routes and release patterns. Read the label, measure liquids accurately and ask a pharmacist before altering a tablet or capsule.

Frequently asked questions

Do all medicines behave in the same way?

No. Effects depend on the ingredient, formulation, route and the patient's circumstances.

Can I change a medicine dose myself?

No. Follow the prescription or label and consult a doctor or pharmacist before making changes.
