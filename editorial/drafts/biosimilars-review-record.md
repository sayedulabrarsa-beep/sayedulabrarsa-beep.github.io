## Bangladesh regulatory status — publication blocker

The following official records do not yet establish one unambiguous current legal instrument:

- WHO Bangladesh's 27 September 2018 report says DGDA launched the Guideline for Registration of Biosimilar Products 2018:
  https://www.who.int/bangladesh/news/detail/27-09-2018-national-guideline-on-the-pharmacovigilance-system-in-bangladesh-launched
- DGDA's public Marketing Authorisation Guidelines page lists a file titled “Guidelines for Registration of Biosimilar Products in Bangladesh 2025”:
  https://dgda.gov.bd/pages/files/6922d9fd933eb65569e019d9
- DGDA's public news listing shows a notice dated 17 August 2025 asking for comments on the *draft* “Biosimilar Product Registration Guideline-2025”:
  https://dgda.gov.bd/pages/news
- DGDA lists DGDA-RIMS as the online registration system for vaccine/biosimilar products:
  https://mail.dgdagov.info/index.php/news/item/24-dgda-regulatory-information-management-system-dgda-rims-for-vaccine-biosimilar-product-online-regestration-system-launched

The public material retrieved in this review does not unambiguously identify the final adoption/effective date of the 2025 document or say whether it formally supersedes the 2018 guideline. A 2026 secondary publication describes a late-2025 update, but it is not a substitute for a definitive DGDA instrument. Do not state the current Bangladesh approval dossier requirements, legal status, interchangeability or pharmacy-level substitution rules until DGDA confirms the applicable final document. The article drafts disclose the source discrepancy and avoid definitive legal conclusions.

## Editorial safeguards

- Distinguish biological medicines from small-molecule generics.
- Do not say biosimilars are molecularly identical.
- Do not imply every biosimilar needs or does not need a comparative efficacy trial; explain the WHO tailored, totality-of-evidence approach.
- Do not imply all biosimilars are automatically interchangeable in every jurisdiction.
- Do not transfer FDA or EU rules to Bangladesh.
- Explain that immunogenicity is product- and patient-specific and that any biological medicine can trigger an immune response.
- Explain that post-marketing traceability should include brand, manufacturer and batch/lot identifiers.
- Keep Bangladesh's 2025 guideline status qualified until DGDA confirms the final version and effective date.
