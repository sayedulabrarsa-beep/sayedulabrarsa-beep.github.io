02Indication

Treatment of iron deficiency in adults (Bangladesh listing).

Some other regional labels describe iron-deficiency anaemia and include patients from 10 years of age. Follow the local approved insert.

03Dosage & administration
  • 30 mg twice daily, preferably on an empty stomach, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals.
  • Do not open, break or chew the capsule.
  • Treatment is generally at least 12 weeks and continues until ferritin is in the normal range.
  • Paediatric: safety and effectiveness are not established in the Bangladesh listing. Some other labels differ, so follow the local insert.
04How it works

Ferric maltol delivers iron for uptake across the intestinal wall and transfer to transferrin and ferritin. It has been shown to raise serum iron parameters, including ferritin and transferrin saturation (TSAT). It is described as a ferric–maltol complex that limits free iron in the gut.

05Interactions
  • Dimercaprol: may raise the risk of nephrotoxicity, so avoid the combination.
  • Some oral drugs (mycophenolate, ethinyl estradiol, ciprofloxacin, doxycycline): bioavailability may fall. Separate doses by at least 4 hours where it matters.
  • Levothyroxine, bisphosphonates, penicillamine: iron may reduce absorption, so separate the doses.
  • Antacids and PPIs: may reduce absorption of iron by altering gastric pH. Avoid together where possible.
  • Food: reduces iron bioavailability. Dairy, tea and coffee do so too.
06Contraindications
  • Known hypersensitivity to ferric maltol or any component.
  • History of haemochromatosis or other iron-overload syndromes.
  • Patients receiving repeated blood transfusions.
  • Anaemia not caused by iron deficiency (per general reference).
07Side effects

Most common: flatulence, diarrhoea, constipation, discoloured (dark) faeces, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting. Dark stools can interfere with occult-blood stool tests.

08Precautions & warnings
  • Avoid during an active inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) flare.
  • Do not give with evidence of iron overload or with intravenous iron.
  • Assess iron parameters before starting and monitor during therapy.
  • Accidental iron overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6.
09Pregnancy, lactation & special populations

After oral use ferric maltol is not absorbed systemically as an intact complex, so fetal exposure and infant exposure through milk are not expected. There are no data on human milk presence, effects on the breastfed child or on milk production. No well-established dose change is defined for renal or hepatic impairment, so use physician oversight.

10Overdose & storage

Overdose. No overdose data are available for Malifa. Early signs of iron overload include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and diarrhoea, and serious cases can progress to systemic toxicity. Seek immediate help.

Storage. Cool place below 25 °C (listing), dry and protected from light. Some references quote below 30 °C, so follow the pack. Keep out of reach of children.

11Frequently asked questions
What is Malifa 30?

Malifa 30 is a capsule containing 30 mg of ferric maltol, an oral iron preparation listed by Navana Pharmaceuticals for iron deficiency in adults.

What is Malifa used for?

Malifa is indicated for the treatment of iron deficiency in adults.

How should Malifa be taken?

The listed dose is 30 mg twice daily, preferably on an empty stomach, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals. The capsule should not be opened, broken or chewed. Always follow your doctor's advice.

How long does iron deficiency treatment take?

Treatment is generally at least 12 weeks and continues until ferritin levels are in the normal range, depending on the severity of iron deficiency.

What are the common side effects of Malifa?

Flatulence, diarrhoea, constipation, discoloured faeces, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting are the most common.

Can children take Malifa?

Safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric patients. Iron overdose is also a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6, so keep it out of their reach.
