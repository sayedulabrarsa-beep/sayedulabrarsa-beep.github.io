Malifa 30

Indications

Malifa capsule is indicated for the treatment of iron deficiency in adults.

Pharmacology and mode of action

Ferric maltol delivers iron for uptake across the intestinal wall and for transfer to transferrin and ferritin. It has been shown to increase serum iron parameters, including ferritin and transferrin saturation (TSAT).

Dosage and administration

  • Take orally, preferably on an empty stomach: at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals.
  • Do not open, break or chew the capsule.
  • Recommended dose: 30 mg twice daily.
  • Duration depends on the severity of iron deficiency. Generally at least 12 weeks of treatment is needed, and treatment continues until ferritin levels are in the normal range.
  • Children and adolescents: safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric patients.

Drug and food interactions

  • Dimercaprol: using iron products together with dimercaprol may increase the risk of nephrotoxicity, so avoid the combination.
  • Other oral drugs: Malifa may reduce the bioavailability of some drugs, including mycophenolate, ethinyl estradiol, ciprofloxacin and doxycycline. Where a reduced effect could matter clinically, separate the doses by at least 4 hours.
  • Food: food decreases the bioavailability of iron from Malifa.

Contraindications

Known hypersensitivity to ferric maltol or any other component; a history of haemochromatosis or other iron-overload syndromes; and patients receiving repeated blood transfusions.

Side effects

The most common side effects are flatulence, diarrhoea, constipation, discoloured faeces, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting.

Pregnancy and lactation

After oral use, ferric maltol is not absorbed systemically as an intact complex, so maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure, and breastfeeding is not expected to expose the child to ferric maltol. There are no data on its presence in human milk, its effect on the breastfed child or its effect on milk production.

Precautions and warnings

  • Avoid Malifa during an active inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) flare, because of a potential risk of increased gastrointestinal inflammation.
  • Do not give to patients with evidence of iron overload or patients receiving intravenous iron.
  • Assess iron parameters before starting and monitor them during therapy.
  • Accidental overdose of iron-containing products is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6 years. Treat accidental overdose immediately.

Overdose

No overdose data are available for Malifa. Early signs of iron overdose may include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and diarrhoea. In more serious cases there may be hypoperfusion, metabolic acidosis and systemic toxicity. Doses above iron needs can lead to iron accumulation in storage sites (haemosiderosis).

Storage

Store in a cool place (below 25°C), dry and protected from light. Keep out of the reach of children.

Frequently asked questions

What is Malifa 30?

Malifa 30 is a capsule containing 30 mg of ferric maltol, an oral iron preparation listed by Navana Pharmaceuticals for iron deficiency in adults.

What is Malifa used for?

Malifa is indicated for the treatment of iron deficiency in adults.

How should Malifa be taken?

The listed dose is 30 mg twice daily, preferably on an empty stomach, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals. The capsule should not be opened, broken or chewed. Always follow your doctor's advice.

How long does iron deficiency treatment take?

Treatment is generally at least 12 weeks and continues until ferritin levels are in the normal range, depending on the severity of iron deficiency.

What are the common side effects of Malifa?

Flatulence, diarrhoea, constipation, discoloured faeces, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting are the most common.

Can children take Malifa?

Safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric patients. Iron overdose is also a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6, so keep it out of their reach.
