Product reference

Finix HP

Rabeprazole Sodium BP 20 mg + Amoxicillin BP 500 mg + Clarithromycin USP 500 mg

Product overview

Finix HP combines an acid-suppressing medicine with two antibacterial medicines. It is intended for a prescribed H. pylori eradication regimen, not for routine acidity, gas or bloating.

Composition

  • Rabeprazole Sodium BP 20 mg tablet
  • Amoxicillin BP 500 mg capsule
  • Clarithromycin USP 500 mg tablet
  • The manufacturer insert describes seven sachets per carton, with one combination blister in each sachet.

Manufacturer-listed use

Eradication of H. pylori in active chronic gastric and duodenal ulcer disease, as stated in the manufacturer's product information.

How the components work

Rabeprazole inhibits the gastric H⁺/K⁺-ATPase proton pump and reduces gastric acid secretion. Amoxicillin interferes with bacterial cell-wall synthesis. Clarithromycin inhibits bacterial protein synthesis through action on the 50S ribosomal subunit.

Important prescribing considerations

Appropriateness depends on confirmed diagnosis, allergy history, previous antibiotic exposure, potential drug interactions, organ function and local antimicrobial resistance patterns. Clarithromycin-containing regimens may not be suitable in every setting. A qualified prescriber must select the regimen using current local guidance. Do not self-start, share or reuse this combination.

Frequently asked questions

What is Finix HP used for?

The manufacturer lists it for H. pylori eradication in specified gastric and duodenal ulcer conditions. It is not a general acidity medicine.

Can Finix HP be taken for ordinary gas or heartburn?

No. It contains two antibiotics and should not be used for ordinary gas, bloating or heartburn without a diagnosis and prescription.

Does everyone with H. pylori need the same treatment?

No. A clinician considers antibiotic resistance, prior antibiotic exposure, allergies, interactions and patient-specific factors when selecting treatment.

Why is antibiotic resistance important?

Resistance can reduce the success of eradication therapy. Treatment should follow current local guidance and the prescriber's assessment.
